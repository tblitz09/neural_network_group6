# Group 6 reflection


## 1 & 4. How much does accuracy change, what are the best and worst results, and what should we report?
Across ten seeds, test accuracy ranges from **65% to 90%**, a **25 percentage-point difference**. Seed 3 is best (90%); seeds 1 and 9 are worst (65%). We would report the **72.25% mean test accuracy**, alongside the range, rather than choosing the luckiest run. The data and split stay the same; changing the initial weights changes the fitted network.

The original single fit in `graph.R` gets **85% test accuracy** and **85.83% training accuracy**. This is separate from the ten-run average.

## 2 & 5. Do the networks agree about tomorrow, and what is their average prediction?
**Nine predict “not up”; one predicts “up,”** using the code’s threshold of greater than 0.5. Their mean predicted score for a rise is **31.31%**, ranging from **0.53% to 73.06%**. Most agree on the direction, but the wide range shows sensitivity to the random start. Nine votes out of ten does not establish 90% confidence.

## 3 & 6. What should the Bank say, and how could the prediction become more trustworthy?
**Press release:** “Nine of our ten networks predict no rise tomorrow, but their predictions vary widely, so the Central Bank considers the forecast uncertain.”

Report the average and spread, test on more unseen data, and repeat training to check consistency. Do not present one favorable run as a guarantee.
